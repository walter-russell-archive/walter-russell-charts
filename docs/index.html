The Russell periodic chart, 1926

A restored facsimile, a measured redraw, and the full ten-octave element table from The Universal One.

Walter Russell published The Universal One in New York in 1926. The book prints a circular chart of the elements and a ten-octave table of 137 rows. This site is a free web edition of both.

What is here

  • The chart — the restored 1926 plate and a measured redraw, in one viewer.
  • The dataset — all 137 rows and 959 cells of the printed table, as an HTML table.
  • The scoreboard — claims printed in the book, each with a verdict and its evidence.
  • Methods — how every image and every cell was produced, step by step.
  • FAQ — direct answers to the questions this chart attracts.
  • Reading order — a suggested path through the material.

Where the images come from

Every image on this site derives from one source: the Library of Congress scan of the 1926 edition, item 27004508. The Library's rights statement covers this collection (retrieved September 11, 2026). It reads: “The books in this collection are in the public domain and are free to use and reuse.” Credit Line: Library of Congress.

Restoration used deterministic image operations only: background flattening, one global threshold, and despeckling. No pixel was drawn by hand or by a model. The redraw was measured from the scan and generated from the transcribed table.

What this edition is not

This edition does not argue that the 1926 book anticipated modern chemistry. Where the book makes a testable claim, the scoreboard tests it and shows the evidence. Verdicts attach to claims, never to people. The plates and the table are presented whole, so readers can check every verdict against the source.
